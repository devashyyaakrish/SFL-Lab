Its central question must always be: **“What changed because of this formula, why did it change, does the formula preserve the core concepts of SFL, and is the change scientifically beneficial under the privacy, communication, computational, statistical, and deployment constraints of Split Federated Learning?”** Every conclusion must be supported by measurable metrics, controlled baselines, graphs, repeated experiments, statistical analysis, SFL-compliance checks, explicit assumptions, clearly identified simulated versus measured quantities, and clearly stated limitations. The application should make complex Split Federated Learning experimentation understandable to students, faculty members, developers, and researchers while retaining sufficient mathematical, algorithmic, experimental, privacy, networking, and statistical detail for serious research work. The final one-line project definition is: **“SFL-Lab is an interactive scientific platform that allows researchers to enter mathematical formulas or algorithms, integrate them into controlled points of a real Split Federated Learning pipeline, and experimentally determine their effects on accuracy, convergence, privacy, communication, Non-IID behaviour, scalability, network robustness, energy consumption and overall SFL correctness through controlled baseline comparison, statistical validation and research-grade visualization.”**
